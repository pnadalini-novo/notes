# Feedback Framework

How to run feedback conversations well - pulled from the old novopayment vault. Reference for 1-1s and performance conversations, not client/team-specific.

## What feedback is (and isn't)

- It's learning, not correction.
- It's not an emotional dump.
- It's not a personal opinion.
- It's not a monologue.
- It's not an annual event - it should be frequent, timely, continuous.

## Types of feedback

- **Reinforcing** - name what's working so it keeps happening.
- **Feedforward** - future-focused: "what could we do differently next time?"
- **Corrective** - adjusts a specific behavior.

1-1 cadence should flex with seniority - more frequent for juniors. Leave room open for growth and learning, not just status.

## Biases to watch for

- **Recency** - overweighting whatever happened most recently.
- **Confirmation** - only noticing evidence that confirms what you already believe.
- **Halo effect** - one strength or weakness colors your whole perception of the person.
- **Affinity** - rating people more highly when they remind you of yourself.

Empathy is putting yourself in the other person's position. Sympathy - just handing them a solution - is not the goal. When a question is hard to answer, resist jumping to solutions; try to understand first.

## Four levels of listening

1. **Download/confirmation** - listening from your own beliefs and judgments ("I already know that").
2. **Factual/understanding** - listening for information you didn't have ("I didn't know...").
3. **Empathic/discovery** - listening to understand their experience and perspective ("now I understand...").
4. **Generative** - listening to build new possibilities together ("what could we do differently?").

## Conversation architecture

1. **Opening** - "I want to talk so I can help you grow" - build trust, create safety.
2. **Observation** - "I noticed that..." - what happened?
3. **Impact** - "This caused..." - why it matters.
4. **Listen** - "How do you see it?" - understand their perspective.
5. **Agreement** - "What will we do differently?" - define the improvement together.

## DRAA model (performance conversations)

Base feedback on objectives, not a simple good/bad verdict.

**Discover** - how they interpret their own development before you give your opinion:
- How do you feel about this period?
- What worked well or not so well?
- What achievement are you most proud of?
- Which objectives do you feel you met best?
- What challenges did you run into?
- What would you do differently if you could repeat this period?
- How do you evaluate your contribution to the team?

**Recognize** - make contributions, strengths, and impact visible - not just results, but collaboration, adaptation, learning, innovation, informal leadership, team contribution.

**Act** - turn the conversation into a development plan (max 3-4 items):
- What's the key learning you're taking away?
- What would you like to do differently?
- What's your biggest growth opportunity?
- What behavior would have the most impact if strengthened?
- What development goal do you want to take on?

**Support** - close as a servant leader:
- What support do you need from me to get there?
- What obstacles could we remove?
- How can I support you?
- What resources would help?
- How often will we check progress?
- Commitment to the leader.

## Useful reframes

- "I don't see it that way" → "Tell me more about how you see it."
- "But that happened because..." → "I understand the context. At the same time, I'd like us to reflect on the impact."
- "Ok" → "What are you thinking right now? What isn't landing?"
- "I feel like my work isn't valued" → "Thanks for sharing how you feel - let's take a moment to understand this better."

**Guide:** don't argue over perceptions; curiosity before argument; listen to the emotion before the content; bring it back to facts.

The goal of all of this: help the other person develop their talent.
